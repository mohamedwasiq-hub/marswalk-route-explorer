          How to play

          1. Check your power. The solar panels make power every sol. A dusty sky means less.
          2. Share it out. Use + and − to make air, grow food, do science or turn on the storm shelter. The heaters always take 1 power at night.
          3. Read the forecast. Mission Control warns you about solar storms. They are usually right, but not always.
          4. End the sol. Your crew of 2 breathes 2 air and eats 2 food. Then see what happened and why.

          Keep air and food above 0 and radiation under the limit for 10 sols. Science earns extra stars.

          Science notes

          For parents, teachers and judges. Every number in the game comes from a NASA record. These are curated, archived facts built into the game, not a live data feed.

          How we scaled it

          A real mission lasts months. To make 10 sols playable, we turned the real values into simple game units:

          • 1 dose point = one sol of normal Mars surface radiation (about 0.64 mSv).
          • A solar storm adds 5 to 9 points. The real storm of May 2024 was the biggest Curiosity had measured, but we keep it small enough that one mistake doesn't end the game.
          • Power cells, air and food are simple units, not kilowatt-hours or kilograms.
          • The forecast is sometimes wrong on purpose. Real space-weather forecasters, like the team behind NASA's DONKI database, can't predict every storm.
