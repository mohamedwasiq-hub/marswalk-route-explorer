    Data & method

    Limitations

    MarsWalk is a research and education prototype. It does not certify any route as safe.

    Terrain only
    It looks at slope and route length. It does not model rocks smaller than the grid, loose sand, dust storms, radiation, temperature, lighting, communications, suit limits or rescue options.
    Resolution
    The elevation model has 1 m posts, and slope is computed over a ~3 m baseline. Boulders, small ledges and cracks under that size are invisible to it.
    Illustrative thresholds
    The 15° "steep" flag and the 25° no-go limit are prototype choices, not NASA EVA standards.
    A relative score
    The TDI compares routes within this area under the priorities you choose. It is not calibrated against real traverses and is not a probability of injury or mission failure.
    Old observations
    The stereo images were taken in 2007. Surface conditions are not current.
    Elevation errors
    HiRISE elevation models can contain artefacts, especially on very steep or shadowed slopes. We don't model this uncertainty yet.
    One small area
    Scoring works only inside the 4.6 × 4.0 km study site. The whole-Mars view is for orientation and has no route analysis.
    No field validation
    Our checks test the software and the data handling (see Data & method). Nothing here has been checked against a real walk on Mars.

    Guide

    A two-minute tour of the map, for anyone new to Mars terrain data.

    Where is this?
    Jezero Crater on Mars, where NASA's Perseverance rover landed in 2021. The bright curving cliff is the front of an ancient river delta. The smooth ground to the east is the crater floor. Press Whole Mars to see where Jezero sits on the planet.
    Elevation
    Height in metres above the "Mars 2000" reference surface (Mars has no sea level). Here everything is about 2.3–2.6 km below that surface, which is why the numbers are negative.
    Slope
    How steep the ground is, in degrees. 0° is flat; 45° rises one metre for every metre forward. Orange and red on the map mean steeper ground.
    Resolution
    Each elevation value covers about 1 × 1 m. We average 3 × 3 of them before computing slope, to reduce noise.
    Route A and Route B
    Route A goes straight. Route B was found by a computer search that avoids slopes over 25° and prefers gentle ground, even if the walk gets longer.
    Terrain Difficulty Index
    One number that mixes slope, extra distance and missing data, using the priorities you set. Change the sliders to see which route wins under different priorities.
    Science stops
    The diamond markers are places worth visiting. Each one shows where its information comes from and how close each route passes.
    Try this
    Drag "Shorter distance" all the way up. Does Route A become the easier choice? Then click the steepest part of Route A to see why it scored badly. Finally, press "Draw a route" and try to beat Route B.
